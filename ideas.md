# Vistara Interior — Design Direction

## Three initial directions

### Theme Name: V1 Editorial Calm
Very generous whitespace, expressive Newsreader typography, and quiet material-led composition. The feeling is considered, residential, and refined rather than promotional.

**Probability:** 0.07

### Theme Name: V2 Photo-Forward
Large daylight interior photography carries the emotional weight, with gallery-like crops, overlaid metadata, and a strong sense of spatial immersion.

**Probability:** 0.03

### Theme Name: V3 Structured Precision
A disciplined layout language brings project specs, material chips, process steps, and corporate trust signals into a clean, legible system.

**Probability:** 0.09

## Chosen direction: Material Editorial Precision

This final direction intentionally combines the strongest elements of all three requested routes rather than presenting separate versions. Editorial typography and generous breathing room establish the premium tone; immersive warm photography creates visual impact; and structured specifications, finish chips, and systemized page templates prove the studio’s precision.

### Design Movement
Contemporary material editorial: the restraint of an architecture monograph, the warmth of a residential journal, and the clarity of a high-end design practice’s project sheet.

### Core Principles
1. **Warmth before spectacle:** daylight, tactile finishes, soft contrast, and a bright Gesso White canvas should feel inviting without becoming rustic or decorative.
2. **Image as evidence:** photography is large and immersive, but always paired with visible project metadata and material details.
3. **Editorial asymmetry:** layouts use offset columns, generous margins, and varied image proportions rather than repetitive centered cards.
4. **Precision in the small details:** mono labels, Oak rules, finish chips, and disciplined spacing make the system feel made, not assembled.

### Color Philosophy
Gesso White keeps the site bright and calm. Limewash creates gentle shifts between editorial sections. Fern is the ownable action and trust color, used for buttons, links, and the in-house factory proof point. Oak and Brass appear as restrained material accents only; they never become a terracotta substitute. Charcoal Ink keeps long-form copy grounded and readable.

### Layout Paradigm
A wide editorial frame with an asymmetrical 7/5 column rhythm. Hero sections pair a text-led column with an offset image plane; project cards alternate image scale and content density; service pages use a consistent split between a strong statement and structured details. On mobile, the composition collapses into a deliberate reading order without losing the image-to-spec relationship.

### Signature Elements
- A 2px Oak rule above every project spec strip.
- Uppercase DM Mono project metadata and finish chips, with the “Made in our factory” chip always in Fern.
- A small Brass square marker and quiet corner labels that make sections feel like pages from a studio folio.

### Interaction Philosophy
Interactions should feel like handling a material sample: responsive, tactile, and restrained. Buttons compress slightly on press, links reveal a Fern underline, images lift by a few pixels on hover, and mobile navigation opens as a calm, full-width panel. No gratuitous motion or dark overlays.

### Animation
Use short 180–260ms ease-out transitions for buttons, links, cards, and navigation. Allow hero copy and image panels to enter with a soft opacity/translate reveal only when motion is permitted. Stagger grouped project cards by 40ms. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` by disabling non-essential entrances and transforms.

### Typography System
Headings use **Newsreader** at medium weight with restrained italic emphasis for key words. Body and UI use **Figtree** for warmth and clarity. Labels, metadata, spec data, and finish chips use **DM Mono**, uppercase with 0.12em tracking. H1s are large but not oversized, with a narrow measure; H2s create editorial pacing; body copy remains comfortably readable.

### Brand Essence
**Vistara Interior is the Bengaluru studio for villa homes and corporate workspaces that want one accountable team, in-house craft, and a warmer interpretation of precision.**

**Personality:** grounded, exacting, welcoming.

### Brand Voice
Headlines are assured and sensory without superlatives. CTAs are direct and consultation-led. Microcopy is specific, calm, and proof-oriented; it never uses generic filler or exclamation marks.

Example lines:
- “Your home, designed around how you live — and built by the same team that answers to corporate deadlines.”
- “A point of view, not a package.”

### Wordmark & Logo
Use the locked text wordmark component: **Vistara** in Newsreader italic paired with the **INTERIOR** DM Mono tag. The mark is kept typographic and editorial in this draft so it can be swapped later without changing the layout system.

### Signature Brand Color
**Fern `#33523E`** — a deep, botanical green that connects the studio’s in-house craft, material intelligence, and dependable delivery.

## Style Decisions

- Keep the brief’s visible `[SAMPLE]`, `[PLACEHOLDER]`, and `[TBC]` markers because they are explicitly locked draft-state content; improve the surrounding hierarchy so the draft state reads as intentional rather than accidental.
- Differentiate service pages through metadata and band labels: residential leans into villa homes and fine apartments, while commercial foregrounds corporate fit-out and named corporate clients.
- Preserve Fern for primary actions and factory proof chips, while keeping secondary links outlined or text-led for a calmer action hierarchy.
- Use the material board as finish evidence, not as a dominant hero; keep the commercial hero architectural and workspace-led, and the residential hero warmer and more domestic.
